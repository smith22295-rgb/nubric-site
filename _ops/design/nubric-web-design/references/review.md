# Design review

Use findings with evidence and a practical fix, rather than a numerical quality score.

| Check | Evidence to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| First impression | The visitor can identify the business, the offer and a next step without scrolling through a pitch. |
| Hierarchy | One primary emphasis per section; headings, body text and supporting labels have distinct roles. |
| Reading | Body text is comfortable on a phone; long lines are limited; essential details are not tiny footnotes. |
| Images | Important content is visible at its rendered size, not stretched or accidentally cropped; real images and demos are accurately described. |
| Navigation | Services have familiar names; the visitor can compare, inspect proof and reach the inquiry form without dead ends. |
| Mobile | Menus fit the viewport, touch actions work, and layout order makes sense without hover. |
| Motion | Movement communicates a change, does not delay content, and respects reduced motion. |
| Consistency | Page intros, buttons, prices, cards and endings feel like one site without every section using the same box. |
| Trust | Prices, inclusions, separate charges and business details agree; portfolio examples do not imply invented outcomes. |
| Delivery | Relevant checks passed, screenshots reviewed, deployed URLs verified, and the previous release remains recoverable. |

Three short tasks for a future real-person review:
1. Find the right service for a small service business and explain what the listed price includes.
2. Find an example of a custom website feature and explain how it could help your own business.
3. Start an inquiry and identify what information you need to send.

Observe hesitation and mistaken assumptions. Do not coach the participant or claim a conversion improvement without evidence.
